Solvable or Not

Every card is one MazeBench room drawn by the game's own renderer, either as the overhead map (the easier tier: every cell is visible) or from the game camera at one of four rotations (the harder tier: tall walls hide cells, as they do for agents). In half of the rooms the green player can reach the gem; in the other half it cannot. Each room has a twin that differs by a single edit and carries the opposite label, and every label comes from the engine's exhaustive solver. Open a card to see both tiers, all rotations and the twin.

The same question on 2D mazes

Generated in the browser from a seed. Each pair has the same walls except at two segments: one keeps a path from S to G, the other does not. Camera rotation does not apply here.

Where the labels come from. A room counts as solvable when the MazeBench engine's A* search finds a move sequence that collects the gem, and as unsolvable when the search has visited every reachable state without finding one. Rooms whose search exceeds its budget are left out. World rooms are the benchmark's own rooms with one wall, gem, box, slope, lift, ice or pit changed; generated rooms are built from scratch with one mechanic each.

The overhead map is the room-preview camera looking straight down: nothing is hidden, so it is the easier tier. The game camera is what agents see, turned in 90° steps as an agent turns it; tall walls hide cells and can hide the gem, so it is the harder tier and the mix of rotations matters.
